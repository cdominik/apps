AggBoard

A notebook whiteboard with a built-in generator for 3D fractal aggregates. Everything is saved automatically on this device.

Getting started

  • Toolbar, from left to right: the … menu (on the right for left-handed use) · pen, eraser, select, laser, particle chain, aggregate box · five ink colours · three line widths · Undo, Redo, spin (show 3D) · page controls: previous / next page, page number, + new page, fit page to screen, and the broom to clear the page.
  • Pencil and fingers: once an Apple Pencil is detected, the pencil draws and fingers move the page (one finger pans, two fingers pinch to zoom). Without a pencil, one finger draws.

Drawing

  • Pen — pressure-sensitive with the Apple Pencil. Choose colour and width in the toolbar.
  • Eraser — removes every stroke it touches (aggregates are deleted via selection instead).
  • Laser — a red trail that fades after a second, for pointing while presenting. It leaves nothing on the page.
  • Straight lines and shapes — at the end of a stroke, keep the pencil still for half a second. The stroke becomes the shape it resembles: a straight line (exactly horizontal, vertical or 45° when close), an angled line such as an L for axes, an arrow, a triangle, a rectangle, another polygon, or a circle or ellipse. You see the shape while holding; lift to keep it, or keep moving to stay freehand. For an arrow, draw the shaft and a quick head in one stroke, or draw a line and then a “V” at its end.
  • Undo / Redo work per page and cover everything: drawing, erasing, moving, clearing and all aggregate changes.

Selecting and moving

  • Select tool: draw a loop around strokes and aggregates, or tap a single item.
  • Tap an aggregate with any tool except the laser to select it.
  • Move: drag inside the selection frame. This works with every tool: whatever is selected moves.
  • Scale a group: drag a corner square. Handwriting keeps its proportions; aggregates keep their exact structure.
  • Selection bar: Duplicate, Delete, SVG (export the selection), and Settings for a single aggregate. Tapping an ink colour recolours selected strokes.
  • Deselect: tap an empty spot (or press Esc).

Aggregates

Choose the aggregate tool and place an aggregate in one of two ways (… → Settings → New aggregates):

  • Circle (dashed-circle icon, the default): put the pencil at the centre and drag sideways. The horizontal distance sets the aggregate's outer radius; moving up makes the monomers bigger, moving down makes them smaller. A ring at the pencil and a second one on the opposite side of the circle show the monomer size; a label shows Df, a and N. To change Df while drawing the circle, draw small loops: clockwise raises it, anticlockwise lowers it (about 0.3 per loop, part of a loop gives part of that; the label shows ↻); size and monomer size stay put while you loop, and a straight movement releases them. With a keyboard: Z lower / X higher, or ↓/↑ (with Shift in finer steps); with a mouse: the scroll wheel. Rest the pencil briefly to see the real aggregate; lift to place it.
  • Box (dashed-box icon): drag a box; when you lift, it fills with a 3D fractal aggregate.

Df scale: while the aggregate tool is active, a Df scale is shown at the screen edge (left, or right for left-handed use) — set Df there (pencil or finger) before drawing. After placing, the scale stays and now changes the new aggregate, as long as it is selected.

Afterwards the tool switches back to the pen and the new aggregate is selected, with three handles:

  • Bottom-right — scale: bigger or smaller with the same structure (the monomers scale too).
  • Bottom-left (dashed box) — refill: change the box; the aggregate is regenerated to fill it at the same monomer size, so the number of monomers changes.
  • Top-right (round arrow) — rotate in 3D. In pan-and-zoom finger mode, dragging one finger on a selected aggregate rotates it too.

Double-tap an aggregate (or select it and tap Settings) for its settings: how it is generated and what is shown on it. Changes appear live while you drag a slider.

  • Method — Cluster–cluster (default) builds it the way real aggregates form, small clusters merging into larger ones; realistic, irregular shapes for Df 1 to 2.3 (above that, particle–cluster is used automatically and the panel says so). Particle–cluster adds monomers one at a time; it covers Df 1 to 3 and keeps the same core when the size changes, while cluster–cluster gives a new structure each time.
  • Monomer size — the monomer radius; the number of monomers adjusts so the aggregate still fills its box (at most 5000).
  • Fractal dimension Df — from 1 (a chain) to 3 (compact).
  • Colour, New random (same settings, different structure), Duplicate, Delete.
  • Advanced — the prefactor kf in N = kf (Rg/a)Df. Auto picks a value that works across the whole Df range.
  • Size spread σg — makes the monomers polydisperse: radii follow a log-normal distribution with geometric standard deviation σg (1 = all equal, up to 1.4), limited to between half and twice the monomer size. The monomer size is then the geometric mean radius ag, and the scaling law N = kf (Rg/ag)Df uses the mass-weighted Rg (each monomer weighted by its volume). While growing, the aggregate follows its accumulated mass (counted in average monomer masses) rather than the monomer count, so that a monomer's size does not decide where it goes; the law then holds on average, typically within a few percent, and for nearly straight chains (Df close to 1) only roughly.
  • Advanced (per aggregate): the prefactor kf (Auto chooses it from Df and the size spread so that the scaling law can be met, or set a fixed value); the subcluster size for cluster–cluster aggregates (Auto ≈ N0.4, or fixed from 2 to 500: small = more hierarchical, closer to pure cluster–cluster aggregation; large = more particle–cluster-like). New aggregates use the values you last set. For a bent string rather than a straight rod, lower Df a little (1.2–1.3) instead of using Df = 1.
  • Show: Numbers — a label below the aggregate with N, Df, kf, the monomer radius a (ag and σg for polydisperse monomers), Rg and the outer radius Rout (in units of a), and the filling factor φ = (total monomer volume)/R3 in units of a, i.e. N (a/R)3 for equal monomers, with R = √(5/3) Rg, the radius of a uniform sphere with the same Rg (so φ ∝ N1−3/Df). Show: Rg circle — a dashed circle of radius Rg around the centre of mass (cross), and a dotted circle at Rout. Show: Growth order / Subclusters — colours the monomers by how the aggregate was built. Particle–cluster: by the order they were added, from purple (first, at the centre) through blue and green to yellow (last, at the rim). Cluster–cluster: each subcluster in its own colour, which shows the patches it was assembled from. Hand-drawn parts of an edited aggregate stay grey. Show: Mass–radius — rings at r = 2a, 4a, 8a, … around one monomer (the one closest to the centre of mass, marked with a dot), each labelled with the number of monomers within that distance in 3D; and a small log-log plot of ⟨N(r)⟩, the same count averaged over many monomers as centres. Its straight part has slope Df: the slope fitted between 3a and 0.7 Rg is shown next to the target Df. At larger r the curve levels off at N. (Averaging matters: the centre of mass of a fluffy aggregate often lies in empty space.) All of these belong to the aggregate: they move, scale and spin with it and appear in exports. New aggregates use your last choice.
  • Readout — N, Rg (in monomer radii a), Df fitted from the growth history as a check, and the anisotropy (largest over smallest principal radius of gyration).

Showing the 3D structure

  • Tap the spin button (next to Undo/Redo, or Space) and the aggregates make one full rotation (8 seconds) and end where they were. Press and hold it (or Shift+Space) to keep them rotating; tap again to stop, and they glide back.
  • … → Settings → Spin: Rock makes the same button rock the aggregates instead: they swing back and forth by about 35° around their orientation (one swing per tap, continuous when held). The small motion shows the depth very clearly, the aggregate never faces away, and arrows or labels you have drawn stay roughly valid. Rotate shows all sides, including the back.
  • If aggregates are selected, only those spin; otherwise all of them on the page. They rotate in step about a slightly tilted axis, so you also see a little of the top and bottom.
  • The spin holds still while the pencil, a finger or the mouse touches the page, so you can point or annotate; it continues when you lift.
  • It is for display only: the orientation you set with the rotate handle is what is saved and exported, and what you get back when the spin ends.

Particle chains

  • The chain tool (spheres without a box) turns a stroke into a chain of touching spheres, at the current monomer size.
  • Connecting: start or end the stroke on a sphere of an aggregate or another chain, and the new chain attaches there in 3D and becomes part of it. Start on one and end on another to bridge them (both need the same monomer size). You can branch off a chain or close it into a ring. New spheres steer around existing ones instead of overlapping. (A selected aggregate is moved instead; tap an empty spot first.)
  • Settings: double-tap a chain to change its monomer size, its temperature (how much it wiggles in 3D; rotate it to see) and its random pattern. It is re-laid along the line you drew. New chains use the last temperature you set.
  • Edited aggregates: an aggregate with attached chains no longer regenerates. Its size slider and corner handle scale it, and Regenerate… turns it back into a fresh aggregate (removing the drawn parts).

Pages and boards

  • … → This page → Board switches between a white page and a dark board, which is easier on the eyes when projecting. New pages get the board of the page you are on. On the dark board, black ink shows as chalk white and the other colours brighter; switching back shows everything exactly as written.
  • … → This page → Format sets the page proportions: Screen (the shape of the drawing area right now, so the whole screen is board), 16:9, 16:10, 4:3 or A4 landscape. All formats have the same width, only the height differs, so switching never moves your drawing sideways. If content ends up below the new bottom edge, a message offers Undo. New pages get the format of the page you are on.
  • Clear a page with the broom at the right end of the toolbar (or … → This page); tap Undo in the message to bring it back. Delete this page removes the page itself and cannot be undone.

Exporting

  • … → Export → This page as image (PNG) — a picture at twice the page size (3200×1800 for 16:9).
  • … → Export → This page as vector graphics (SVG) — sharp at any size; opens in browsers, Inkscape, Illustrator, Keynote and PowerPoint.
  • For figures: select what you want and tap SVG in the selection bar. Only the selection is exported, cropped, on a transparent background (dark-board pages keep their dark background).

Saving, sharing, offline

  • Notebooks are stored in this browser on this device, automatically. Different browsers (and the Home Screen app) keep separate notebooks.
  • On iPad, add AggBoard to the Home Screen: Safari may otherwise delete a website's data after a week without use.
  • … → Notebook → Save notebook to file for backups, to move a notebook to another browser or device, or to give it to someone; they use Open notebook from file.
  • Offline: after one visit with a network connection, AggBoard starts without network. When a new version has been downloaded, a message with Reload appears; nothing changes until you tap it.

Settings, mouse and keyboard

  • … → Settings → Writing hand: with Left, the … button, the menu and the Df scale move to the right side, away from your writing hand.
  • … → Settings → Finger input: Auto (fingers draw until a pencil is detected), Draw (one finger always draws), Pan & zoom (one finger always moves the page). Your palm is ignored while the pencil is on the screen.
  • Mouse / trackpad: click to draw or select, double-click an aggregate for its settings, right- or middle-drag to pan, two-finger swipe to pan, pinch or Ctrl + wheel to zoom.
  • Keys: P pen · E eraser · S select · L laser · C chain · A aggregate · 1–5 colours · [ / ] thinner / thicker line · Ctrl/⌘ Z undo · Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z redo · Ctrl/⌘ D duplicate · Delete delete selection · PgUp/PgDn pages · Z/X or ↓/↑ lower / higher Df while placing a circle aggregate · 0 fit page · Space one rotation or swing / stop, Shift+Space keep going · Esc close menus and dialogs, or deselect.

If something goes wrong

  • Version: shown at the bottom of the … menu.
  • “Update incomplete”: some files on the web server are from an older version. Check that all files were uploaded, wait a few minutes, then reload.
  • Pencil problems: open … → Help → Input diagnostics, reproduce the problem, tap Copy and send the text. It records what the device reports and what the app did with it; nothing is sent anywhere by itself.
