AggBoard

A notebook whiteboard with a built-in generator for 3D fractal aggregates. Everything is saved automatically on this device.

Getting started

  • Toolbar, from left to right: the … menu and the Pencil/finger button (both on the right for left-handed use) · pen, eraser, select, laser, particle chain, aggregate · five ink colours · three line widths · Undo, Redo, spin (show 3D) · page controls: previous / next page, the page number (tap it for the page overview), + new page, fit page to screen, and the broom to clear the page.
  • Pencil and fingers: the button next to … shows who draws. Pencil symbol: the Pencil draws, one finger pans, two fingers pinch to zoom, and your palm is ignored. Finger symbol (tinted): one finger draws. Tap the button to switch, for example when you put the Pencil away. As soon as the Pencil touches the screen, it switches back to the Pencil by itself (a short message says so). Without a Pencil, fingers draw from the start.

Drawing

  • Pen — pressure-sensitive with the Apple Pencil. Choose colour and width in the toolbar.
  • Eraser — removes every stroke it touches (aggregates are deleted via selection instead).
  • Laser — a red trail that fades after a second, for pointing while presenting. It leaves nothing on the page.
  • Straight lines and shapes — at the end of a stroke, keep the pencil still for half a second. The stroke becomes the shape it resembles: a straight line (exactly horizontal, vertical or 45° when close), an angled line such as an L for axes, an arrow, a triangle, a rectangle, another polygon, or a circle or ellipse. You see the shape while holding; lift to keep it, or keep moving to stay freehand. For an arrow, draw the shaft and a quick head in one stroke, or draw a line and then a “V” at its end.
  • Undo / Redo work per page and cover everything on it: drawing, erasing, moving, clearing and all aggregate changes. Deleting a whole page is undone with the Undo in the message that appears.

Selecting and moving

  • Select tool: draw a loop around strokes and aggregates, or tap a single item.
  • Tap an aggregate with any tool except the laser to select it.
  • Move: drag inside the selection frame. This works with every tool: whatever is selected moves.
  • Scale a group: drag a corner square. Handwriting keeps its proportions; aggregates keep their exact structure.
  • Selection bar: Duplicate, Delete, SVG and Animation (export the selection), and Settings for a single aggregate or chain. Tapping an ink colour recolours selected strokes.
  • Deselect: tap an empty spot (or press Esc).

Aggregates

Choose the aggregate tool and place an aggregate in one of two ways (… → Settings → Aggregates: New):

  • Circle (dashed-circle icon, the default): put the pencil at the centre and drag sideways. The horizontal distance sets the aggregate's outer radius; moving up makes the monomers bigger, moving down makes them smaller. A ring at the pencil and a second one on the opposite side of the circle show the monomer size; a label shows Df, a and N. To change Df while drawing the circle, draw small loops: clockwise raises it, anticlockwise lowers it (about 0.3 per loop, part of a loop gives part of that; the label shows ↻); size and monomer size stay put while you loop, and a straight movement releases them. With a keyboard: Z lower / X higher, or ↓/↑ (with Shift in finer steps); with a mouse: the scroll wheel. Rest the pencil briefly to see the real aggregate; lift to place it.
  • Box (dashed-box icon): drag a box; when you lift, it fills with a 3D fractal aggregate.

Df scale: while the aggregate tool is active, a Df scale is shown at the screen edge (left, or right for left-handed use) — set Df there (pencil or finger) before drawing. After placing, the scale stays and now changes the new aggregate, until you deselect it; after that, change its Df in its Settings.

Afterwards the tool switches back to the one you used before, and the new aggregate is selected, with three handles:

  • Bottom-right — scale: bigger or smaller with the same structure (the monomers scale too).
  • Bottom-left (dashed box) — refill: change the box; the aggregate is regenerated to fill it at the same monomer size, so the number of monomers changes.
  • Top-right (round arrow) — rotate in 3D. While fingers move the page (Pencil symbol on the button), dragging one finger on a selected aggregate rotates it too.

Double-tap an aggregate (or select it and tap Settings) for its settings, from top to bottom. Changes appear live while you drag a slider, and each change is one Undo step.

  • Method — Cluster–cluster (default) builds it the way real aggregates form, small clusters merging into larger ones; realistic, irregular shapes for Df 1 to 2.3 (above that, particle–cluster is used automatically and the panel says so). Particle–cluster adds monomers one at a time; it covers Df 1 to 3 and keeps the same core when the size changes, while cluster–cluster gives a new structure each time.
  • Fractal dimension Df — from 1 (a straight chain) to 3 (compact). For a bent string rather than a straight rod, use 1.2–1.3.
  • Monomer size — the monomer radius a; the number of monomers adjusts so the aggregate still fills its circle or box (at most 5000).
  • Size spread σg — makes the monomers polydisperse: radii follow a log-normal distribution with geometric standard deviation σg (1 = all equal, up to 1.4), limited to between half and twice the monomer size, which is then the geometric mean radius ag. The scaling law uses the mass-weighted Rg (each monomer weighted by its volume). While growing, the aggregate follows its accumulated mass rather than the monomer count, so that a monomer's size does not decide where it goes; the law then holds on average, typically within a few percent, and for nearly straight chains (Df close to 1) only roughly.
  • Readout — N, Rg (in monomer radii a), Df fitted from the growth history as a check, and the anisotropy R1/R3 (largest over smallest principal radius of gyration).
  • Show — four buttons that draw information on the aggregate (tinted = on). They move, scale and spin with it and appear in exports; new aggregates use your last choice.
    • Numbers: a small table below the aggregate: the kind of aggregate, then N, Df, kf, the monomer radius a (ag and σg for polydisperse monomers), Rg and the outer radius Rout (in units of a), and below a line the filling factor φ and the anisotropy R1/R3 (largest over smallest principal radius of gyration). The size of all such text (labels, plot, ring numbers) is set in … → Settings → Aggregates: Text. The filling factor φ = (total monomer volume)/R3 in units of a, i.e. N (a/R)3 for equal monomers, with R = √(5/3) Rg, the radius of a uniform sphere with the same Rg (so φ ∝ N1−3/Df).
    • Rg circle: a dashed circle of radius Rg around the centre of mass (cross), and a dotted circle at Rout.
    • Growth order (particle–cluster) or Subclusters (cluster–cluster): colours the monomers by how the aggregate was built — by the order they were added, from purple (first, at the centre) through blue and green to yellow (last, at the rim), or each subcluster in its own colour, which shows the patches it was assembled from. Hand-drawn parts of an edited aggregate stay grey.
    • Mass–radius: rings at r = 2a, 4a, 8a, … around the monomer closest to the centre of mass (marked with a dot), each labelled with the number of monomers within that distance in 3D; and a small log-log plot of ⟨N(r)⟩, the same count averaged over many monomers as centres. Its straight part has slope Df: the slope fitted between 3a and 0.7 Rg is shown next to the target Df. At larger r the curve levels off at N. (Averaging matters: the centre of mass of a fluffy aggregate often lies in empty space.)
  • Colour — the sphere colour.
  • Advanced — the prefactor kf in N = kf (Rg/a)Df (Auto chooses it from Df and the size spread so that the scaling law can be met, or set a fixed value), and the subcluster size for cluster–cluster aggregates (Auto ≈ N0.4, or fixed from 2 to 500: small = more hierarchical, closer to pure cluster–cluster aggregation; large = more particle–cluster-like). These belong to the aggregate; new aggregates use the values you last set.
  • New random (same settings, a different structure; for an edited aggregate Remove chains… instead, see Particle chains) · Duplicate · Coordinates… · Delete.
  • Coordinates… saves the monomers as a text file, one line per monomer: index radius x y z, in units of the monomer radius (for a size spread: of the geometric mean radius), in the orientation shown on the board (x right, y up, z toward you), centred on the centre of mass. Header lines start with # and record N, Df, kf, method, seed and Rg; the file reads directly with numpy.loadtxt, Matlab, gnuplot or Fortran.

Showing the 3D structure

  • Tap the spin button (next to Undo/Redo, or Space) and the aggregates make one full rotation (8 seconds) and end where they were. Press and hold it (or Shift+Space) to keep them rotating; tap again to stop, and they glide back.
  • … → Settings → Aggregates: Spin: Rock makes the same button rock the aggregates instead: they swing back and forth by about 35° around their orientation (one swing per tap, continuous when held). The small motion shows the depth very clearly, the aggregate never faces away, and arrows or labels you have drawn stay roughly valid. Rotate shows all sides, including the back.
  • As an animation file: … → Export: Animation, or select some aggregates and tap Animation in the selection bar (cropped to the selection, with room for the turning aggregates). Choose the format, rotate or rock, size and smoothness; you get a preview, then Save…. Both formats contain exactly one cycle. Video (MP4) is small and sharp and plays in PowerPoint and Keynote (set it to loop there); GIF repeats by itself and works anywhere, but its files are much larger. Video needs a browser with WebCodecs (current Chrome, Edge, Safari, and every browser on a current iPad).
  • If aggregates are selected, only those spin; otherwise all of them on the page. They rotate in step about a slightly tilted axis, so you also see a little of the top and bottom.
  • The spin holds still while the pencil, a finger or the mouse touches the page, so you can point or annotate; it continues when you lift.
  • It is for display only: the orientation you set with the rotate handle is what is saved and exported, and what you get back when the spin ends.

Particle chains

  • The chain tool (a row of spheres) turns a stroke into a chain of touching spheres, at the current monomer size.
  • Connecting: start or end the stroke on a sphere of an aggregate or another chain, and the new chain attaches there in 3D and becomes part of it. Start on one and end on another to bridge them, also between different monomer sizes. You can branch off a chain or close it into a ring. New spheres steer around existing ones instead of overlapping. (A selected aggregate is moved instead; tap an empty spot first.)
  • Settings: double-tap a chain for the same panel as for aggregates, with monomer size and temperature (how much it wiggles in 3D; rotate or spin it to see). New random gives a new random wiggle. The chain is re-laid along the line you drew. New chains use the last temperature you set.
  • Edited aggregates: an aggregate with attached chains no longer regenerates. Its size slider and corner handle scale it, and Remove chains… removes the drawn parts and rebuilds the aggregate from its settings: the same structure as before you attached anything (a message offers Undo).

Pages and boards

  • Page overview: tap the page number in the toolbar (or … → Notebook: Pages) to see all pages as thumbnails. Tap one to go there. To reorder, hold a thumbnail for a moment until it lifts, then drag it; a blue bar shows where it will go (near the top or bottom edge the overview scrolls along; Esc cancels). A quick swipe scrolls, a tap opens the page. Under each thumbnail: move earlier, move later, duplicate (a full copy right after it) and delete. New page adds an empty page at the end.
  • … → This page → Board switches between a white page and a dark board, which is easier on the eyes when projecting. New pages get the board of the page you are on. On the dark board, black ink shows as chalk white and the other colours brighter; switching back shows everything exactly as written.
  • … → This page → Format sets the page proportions: Screen (the shape of the drawing area right now, so the whole screen is board), 16:9, 16:10, 4:3 or A4 landscape. All formats have the same width, only the height differs, so switching never moves your drawing sideways. If content ends up below the new bottom edge, a message offers Undo. New pages get the format of the page you are on.
  • Clear a page with the broom at the right end of the toolbar (or … → This page: Clear page); tap Undo in the message to bring it back. Delete page (in the menu or the page overview) removes the page itself; the message that follows also offers Undo.

Exporting

  • … → Export: PNG — the whole page as a picture at twice the page size (3200×1800 for 16:9).
  • … → Export: SVG — the page as vector graphics, sharp at any size; opens in browsers, Inkscape, Illustrator, Keynote and PowerPoint.
  • … → Export: Animation — the spin as a video or GIF (see Showing the 3D structure).
  • For figures: select what you want and tap SVG (or Animation) in the selection bar. Only the selection is exported, cropped. The SVG has a transparent background (dark-board pages keep their dark background); animations always have the page background.

Saving, sharing, offline

  • Notebooks are stored in this browser on this device, automatically. Different browsers (and the Home Screen app) keep separate notebooks.
  • On iPad, add AggBoard to the Home Screen: Safari may otherwise delete a website's data after a week without use.
  • … → Notebook: Save to file for backups, to move a notebook to another browser or device, or to give it to someone; they use Open file.
  • Offline: after one visit with a network connection, AggBoard starts without network. When a new version has been downloaded, a message with Reload appears; nothing changes until you tap it.

Settings, mouse and keyboard

  • … → Settings opens the settings inside the menu (‹ Menu goes back): writing hand, how new aggregates are placed (circle or box), spin style (rotate or rock), the size of the text drawn on aggregates (numbers, plot, ring labels; also in exports), and input diagnostics. Whether fingers draw is set with the button next to … (see Getting started).
  • Hand: with Left, the … and Pencil/finger buttons, the menu and the Df scale move to the right side, away from your writing hand.
  • Mouse / trackpad: click to draw or select, double-click an aggregate for its settings, right- or middle-drag to pan, two-finger swipe to pan, pinch or Ctrl + wheel to zoom.
  • Keys: P pen · E eraser · S select · L laser · C chain · A aggregate · 1–5 colours · [ / ] thinner / thicker line · Ctrl/⌘ Z undo · Ctrl/⌘ Shift Z redo · Ctrl/⌘ D duplicate · Delete delete selection · PgUp/PgDn previous / next page · Z/X or ↓/↑ lower / higher Df while placing a circle aggregate · 0 fit page · Space one rotation or swing / stop, Shift+Space keep going · Esc close menus, dialogs and the page overview, or deselect.

If something goes wrong

  • Version: shown at the bottom of the … menu.
  • “Update incomplete”: some files on the web server are from an older version. Check that all files were uploaded, wait a few minutes, then reload.
  • Pencil problems: open … → Settings → Input diagnostics, reproduce the problem, tap Copy and send the text. It records what the device reports and what the app did with it; nothing is sent anywhere by itself.
