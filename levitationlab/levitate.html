LEVITATION LAB

A real laboratory experiment, in playable form.

The ERC funded project GT4Pebbles, Ground Truth for Pebbles, is a program to study how planet formation begins in disks around young stars. In our lab, we levitate dust particles in a rotating drum for extended periods of time to allow them to collide and grow.

This simulation captures the physics of that experiment — the same equations, the same trade-offs — and lets you explore them yourself. Once the experiment is running in the real world, we will also expose real lab data in this game.

Particles fall through a thin gas. Spin the drum, and the gas drags them into orbits. Hold them in the highlighted zone long enough and they become levitated. You can explore yourself, or you can enter GAME mode to walk you through a series of increasingly difficult challenges to make you master of the levitation drum.

Click anywhere (except the checkbox) to continue.

LEVITATION LAB · Manual

The drum is a cylindrical vacuum chamber viewed head-on. Particles are injected from the brass manifold above the drum and fall through rarified gas toward the bottom wall. By rotating the drum, you rotate the gas with it, which drags particles into orbits. The goal is to levitate as many particles as possible in the highlighted region on the right of the drum.

How to play

  • Set up the run with the left wing: number of particles (1–10000), settling velocity vt, optional spread of vt across particles, and the time interval over which particles are injected. Particles with larger vt appear visibly larger.
  • Inject particles with the right-wing button. You can start rotating the drum before or after injection.
  • Rotate the drum by swiping across it. To slow down, swipe in the opposite direction. The Ω lock button on the right wing freezes the current rotation rate.
  • Particles that hit the wall become red (LOST). Particles floating are yellow. A particle that stays in the highlighted region for at least one full rotation period turns green and counts as LEVITATED.
  • Reset returns the drum to a clean state, ready for a new injection.

Optional instruments

  • Sound — motor hum (pitch tracks rotation speed), a dull plonk when a particle is lost, and a soft chime when one becomes levitated.
  • Trails — each particle leaves a fading trail of its recent path, so orbits become visible. Disabled at very high particle counts.
  • Laser — engages lidar mode: the drum interior goes dark and particles become invisible. A scanning green laser beam sweeps the chamber, briefly revealing any particles it crosses.
  • Lighting — toggles the lab between a dim night setting and a bright day setting.
  • Microscope / Chart — slide the ceiling-mounted viewport down to display close-up imagery (microscope) or data plots (chart). That data actually comes from our real lab and simulations.

Game & Challenge

  • Game Mode — Progress through predefined levels with locked parameters. You must levitate a target number of particles for a set duration to clear the level.
  • Challenge Mode — A high-score competition. Using the current parameter setup, levitate as many particles as you can for 3 full orbits, then enter your name on the local leaderboard.

Expert Panel

The expert panel sits behind a locked door at the bottom of the left wing. Its controls expose the underlying physics and analytical tooling, and are grouped into three sections: HUD (visualisation overlays), Processes (toggles for the physics of growth), and System (simulation-wide settings).

HUD — visualisation overlays

  • Orbit centre projection — overlays the range of orbit centres implied by the current particle vt distribution and drum rotation. Shows where particles should be circling given the current settings. Updates live as you change vt, spread, drum speed, or injection size distribution profile.
  • Flash orbit of new aggregates — when an aggregate forms from particles, briefly draws its orbit ring alongside the mean-vt orbit of the levitated population. Useful for seeing the small Stokes-number bump aggregates inherit from their vt factor.
  • Aggregate encounter projections — for every pair of live aggregates, computes the closest approach position during the next orbit and draws the two aggregates at those positions with their collisional cross-sections. Brownian perturbations cause the projections to shift until a collision occurs.
  • vt distribution — draws two kernel-density curves inside the drum: a yellow curve for all floating particles currently inside the drum (not yet levitated), and a green curve for levitated particles. An amber histogram shows the terminal velocities of live aggregates. All three are normalised independently.
  • Aggregate size distribution — draws a live histogram inside the left half of the drum showing the current population of aggregates by monomer count (bins 10–100 in steps of 10) plus a gold bar for pebbles.
  • Backdrop — slides an opaque sheet behind all active analytical overlays for extra contrast against the drum interior. Short press toggles it on and off; long press opens a slider to set the opacity continuously from 0 to 100%.
  • Analysis projections — the central HUD button powers on a heatmap engine that accumulates data over full drum revolutions. The flanking ◀ ▶ arrows cycle through six modes: vector field, ghost paths, representative orbits, particle density, velocity variance, and a collision proxy (density × variance). In ghost paths mode a particle is automatically selected and three predicted future paths are shown: the actual orbit (white solid), the gas-only co-rotation path (cyan dashed), and the vacuum free-fall path (purple dashed). Click any particle to select it instead. A colour bar and caption appear while the HUD is active.

Processes — toggle the physics of growth

  • Nanocoating — coats the drum wall so particles never stick. Lets you study orbits and accumulate large populations without attrition.
  • Sweep — removes all non-levitated floating particles instantly. Press once to clean up the drum without a full reset.
  • Widen slice — duplicates all levitated particles and aggregates, placing each copy 90° ahead in its orbit. Models widening the simulated drum slice to include a neighbouring region that has evolved identically, using computational headroom lost to wall collisions and aggregate formation.
  • Pure average vt — when on, aggregates inherit the exact mass-weighted mean vt of their constituent monomers with no Stokes-number bump. Useful for comparing the effect of the vt factor on aggregate orbit positions.
  • Aggregate formation — green when on (default), red when off. Disabling halts the levitated particles → aggregate transition entirely, allowing you to accumulate a large levitated population without it self-organising.
  • Growth mode — a three-state button cycling green → amber → red on each press:
    • Green (Pebble) — normal operation: seven levitated aggregates merge into a pebble after holding for the required revolutions.
    • Amber (Grow) — aggregate-aggregate collision growth is active. Aggregates undergo Brownian motion and merge when they overlap. Each merge produces a larger aggregate using the mass-weighted vt. An aggregate reaching 100 monomers automatically becomes a pebble. Pebble formation via the standard seven-aggregate path is disabled in this mode. The orbit flash HUD (if active) shows the two parent orbits fading out in orange and the new merged orbit brightening in cyan.
    • Red (Off) — all growth beyond aggregate formation is disabled. Aggregates accumulate indefinitely.
  • Slow-motion during merging — when armed, the simulation runs at one-fifth speed during all merge animations (aggregate formation, aggregate growth, pebble formation), so you can watch them happen in detail.
  • Fast chain — accelerated synthesis with lower thresholds. Only for the game designer as it bypasses the discovery arc of the game.

System — simulation-wide settings

  • Injection profile — opens a panel to configure the particle vt distribution: the standard Gaussian (driven by the wing sliders), a bi-disperse mixture with two independently configurable groups and a mixing ratio, or a power-law distribution between vmin and vmax with index q. New profiles apply to the next injection event. The VT and Spread displays on the left wing show a coloured label when a custom profile is active and can be clicked to get to the panel again.
  • Auto-omega — continuously sets the drum speed so that the full range of orbit centres for the current vt injection distribution is centred inside the levitation zone. Updates in real time as you change vt, spread, or injection profile. Pairs well with the orbit centre projection overlay to verify the centring visually.
  • Collect — arms the collection tray. When armed, the tray waits until the slot marker on the rim reaches the top of the drum, then slides a metal blade across the interior, scooping up everything floating inside (particles, aggregates, and pebbles) onto the blade. The drum decelerates to a stop with the blade resting horizontally. Press again before insertion begins to disarm.
  • Simulation speed — a five-position gearbox (¼× / ½× / 1× / 2× / 4×). The middle button shows the current gear and snaps back to 1× when pressed. Speed affects all physics including drum decay and particle injection timing.
  • Stroboscopic mode — the canvas repaints only once per full drum revolution rather than every frame.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space — pause and resume the simulation. The drum rotation, all physics, and the motor sound freeze together. The canvas continues to render the frozen state.

URL shortcuts

  • ?expert — open the expert door automatically and activates the Ω lock for quick, deep testing during development.
  • ?game — start Game Mode at level 1, skipping the splash. Append a level number (e.g. ?game=3) to start at that level.
  • ?challenge — start Challenge Mode with the built-in challenge parameters (300 particles, 30% spread). Override selectively with ?challenge&np=1000, &vt=40, &spread=0.20, or &dt=5.
  • ?modern — apply the clinical lab visual theme.
  • ?pfeiffer — apply the clinical lab visual theme with red support structures pointing to the Pfeiffer company building the real world setup.
  • ?verify — developer smoke-test mode: 3000 particles, 30% spread, no Ω decay.

The expert door can also be opened by triple-tapping the title plate.

Press Esc or × to close.
